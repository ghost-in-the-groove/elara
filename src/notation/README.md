# elara_notation

The notation engine (ADR-0011): it turns the `elara_core` model into what a
reader sees. Lengths are in staff spaces; renderers (the app's Qt scene graph,
SVG and PDF) choose how big a staff space is.

| Header | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| `display_list.hpp` | What layout produces and renderers draw: `GlyphItem` (at the staff's size or `scale`d), `LineItem` (solid or dashed), `BeamItem`, `TextItem`, `CurveItem` and `PolygonItem` in staff spaces (y down, top line of the first staff at 0), each with a `Role` and its measure; `SystemBox` per system, `StaffBox` per staff, `MeasureBox` per measure and `ColumnBox` per column (where the notes starting at a time in a measure are placed); `TextFace` (regular, italic, bold: the tempo and the names of sections, ADR-0133) and `textWidth()`, the text metrics layout assumes in each face. |
| `staff_layout.hpp` | `layoutSystem()`: staves in standard notation or tablature (`StaffSelection`, `StaffView`) on one line, one under the other, notes that start together aligned (ADR-0013). Standard notation: clef, key and time signatures, noteheads at their written pitch, accidentals, dots, ledger lines, stems, flags, beams, ties, articulations, rests and barlines; tuplet numbers and brackets (ADR-0040); dynamics marks under the staff where the loudness changes (ADR-0044) and the wedges of hairpins between them (ADR-0078); double barlines in the place of a measure's single one unless a repeat barline or the final barline stands there (ADR-0103); repeat barlines, the play count of repeats played more than twice and the brackets of alternate endings above the first staff (ADR-0016), segno, coda and jump texts above them (ADR-0049), tempo marks above those (ADR-0042), with "accel." or "rit." and a dashed line after a gradual change (ADR-0082) and, where the triplet feel changes, the pair as written equal to the pair as played (ADR-0104), and the names of sections, boxed, above everything (ADR-0063); drum staves through the default drum map (ADR-0012); a brace left of the staves of a grand staff (two or more staves of one track in standard notation, such as a piano's) and barlines through them (ADR-0073); the strums of instruments neither fretted nor percussion as arpeggio lines left of their chords (ADR-0076); grace notes small before their chords, slashed before the beat, beamed when several (ADR-0079); chord symbols above everything of their staff, the names on one baseline and, on staves with frets, diagrams under them (ADR-0080), and beat texts in italics above them, each starting at its beat, on rows upwards when they would run into each other (ADR-0081); lyrics right under the staff's notes, a line per verse, each syllable centred under its note (from the notehead when held), hyphens between the syllables of a word and lines under held notes, the notes making room for the syllables and the dynamics going under them (ADR-0084); tenuto lines by the notehead, beyond the staccato dot or the staccatissimo wedge in its place, each in a space inside the staff (ADR-0088, ADR-0094); breath marks over the staff and caesuras through its top line after their beats, which make room for them (ADR-0095); octave lines over (8va, 15ma) or under (8vb, 15mb) the notes they write nearer the staff, the numeral clear of the ink, dashes to the last notehead and a hook, cut at the end of a system and repeated at the start of the next (ADR-0089); trills: "tr" centred over the notehead, clear of the ink, under the staff for the lower voice of two, and a wavy line on to the next column when there is room (ADR-0090); mordents and turns placed as "tr" is (ADR-0091); fingerings one over the other over the notes, under them on a lower staff of the track and for the lower voice of two, and for a fretted instrument small and left of the noteheads with the plucking fingers over the notes (ADR-0093); the sustain pedal under the staff, below the dynamics: "Ped." at its first beat, a line on to where it comes up and a hook, a notch at each change, over barlines and cut at the end of a system, the notes making room for the notches and the hooks (ADR-0092); fermatas centred over their notes or rests, clear of the stems, under the staff for the lower voice of two (ADR-0085); the notes of voices that start together a second apart side by side with their stems in one line, a unison of the same notehead and dots shared, other unisons side by side, the lower voice's note on the right with its accidental moved back left (ADR-0151); slurs from the first beat slurred in a row to the beat after the last, on the notehead side when every stem goes up and above otherwise (above or below for the voices of several), arching over the notes between and cut where a line ends (ADR-0086), and over the notes hammer-ons, pull-offs and legato slides join, from a grace note to its note too (ADR-0097); slides as straight lines from notehead to notehead, cut at the end of a system and going on at the start of the next, and short lines into and out of a note (ADR-0098); ghost notes' noteheads between parentheses, their accidentals outside on the left and dots after them (ADR-0099); dead notes as crosses and natural harmonics as diamonds (ADR-0100); tremolo picking as slashes through the stem, which grows to fit them (ADR-0101); clef changes: each measure laid out in the clef in force there, the new clef smaller before the barline of the measure before (at the end of a line too) and in full at the start of the next line (ADR-0087); measure repeat signs instead of a measure rest, on notation and tablature: one measure's in its middle, a two-measure repeat's over the barline between its measures, or in each when a line ends between them (ADR-0102). Tablature: fret numbers large enough to read from arm's length and, only when it stands alone, the time signature (ADR-0113), with dead, ghost, tied and harmonic notes, grace notes' fret numbers small before them (ADR-0079), hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides and bends, and lanes for palm mute, let ring, vibrato, trills ("tr" and the other note's fret, ADR-0090) and mordents and turns (ADR-0091), harmonics, pick strokes, strums and tremolo picking, the whammy bar (its curve and depth, "w/bar" at the start of a run) and tapping, slap and pop (ADR-0061); without standard notation of the staff, its rests and its rhythm under it (stems, beams, flags, dots and tuplets, ADR-0041), its dynamics marks and hairpins, its chord symbols, its beat texts, its lyrics and its fermatas. `layoutStaff()`: one standard staff. `selectTracks()`: the staves of whole tracks as notation, tablature or both (`TrackView`), tablature only where a staff has a tuning; `hasTablature()`. |
| `page_layout.hpp` | `layoutPages()`: the same staves broken into systems that fill the width of a page, justified, and stacked on pages with the title and credits, measure numbers, track names and page numbers (ADR-0014); `PageLayoutOptions` (page size in staff spaces, margins, measures per system, threads), `pageOptions()` for A4 or Letter (`kA4`, `kLetter`) at a staff space in millimetres. Systems are laid out on every core. `cutIntoStrips()` cuts a page as long as the music into strips of whole systems, no higher than a given height, that stacked without gaps are the page (the continuous view, ADR-0057). |
| `staff_size.hpp` | `fittedStaffSpace()`: the staff space for the staves shown, the largest up to the one asked for that leaves two systems a page, never under `kLeastStaffShare` of it nor `kLeastStaffSpace`; `typicalSystemHeight()`, estimated from the staves alone so that editing the music never changes it (ADR-0171) |
| `page_layout.hpp` | `PageLayoutCache`: lays out the versions of a song as it is edited, each as `layoutPages()` would, measuring and laying out again only the measures and systems an edit changed (ADR-0052) |
| `svg.hpp` | `toSvg()`: a display list as a standalone SVG document. |
| `pdf.hpp` | `toPdf()`: display lists as the pages of one PDF document (ADR-0074), glyphs drawn from their outlines, text in Helvetica, streams compressed by the compressor the caller passes (`PdfOptions`). |
| `font_outlines.hpp` | `FontOutlines`: the outline of each `Glyph` (moves, lines and cubic curves in staff spaces) read from an OpenType font with CFF outlines such as `Bravura.otf`, by running its Type 2 charstrings (ADR-0074). |
| `caret.hpp` | Keyboard entry on a laid-out score (ADR-0032): `caretBox()`, where a caret shows (over the fret number on its string, on a drum lane where the drum map places its kit piece, or across a standard staff; the free slot between the columns around its time), `caretColumn()`, the band that says its beat across every view of its staff in the system (ADR-0132), and `caretAt()`, the caret a click picks (the nearest string or drum lane, ADR-0035). |
| `symbol.hpp` | Picking a symbol with the pointer (ADR-0130): `symbolAt()` gives the symbol of the roles asked for that is drawn at a point, or within `kPickSlack` of it: its role, the measure it was laid out for, the box round its ink (a text's where layout and the layout issues assume it is) and the staff it is on. A symbol is everything of one role drawn together for one measure (the sharps of a key signature, the note and the number of a tempo mark). `kEditedRoles` are the ones that are edited where they are drawn. |
| `layout_issues.hpp` | What laid-out pages could not keep (ADR-0150): `layoutIssues()` and `overlaps()` give each `LayoutIssue`, ink of two roles that must never touch (`mustNotTouch()`: noteheads, accidentals, fret numbers, syllables, chord names, dynamics) going into each other by more than `kOverlapTolerance`, or a measure wider than the page. A sweep per system over finished pages; layout is not changed. |
| `smufl.hpp` | SMuFL music fonts: `Glyph`, the symbols Elara draws, with their SMuFL names and code points (`glyphName()`, `codepoint()`, `glyphNamed()`); `EngravingFont`, read from a font's SMuFL metadata (`fromMetadata()`, `fromMetadataFile()`), with each glyph's bounding box, advance width and anchors (`metrics()`) and the font's engraving defaults (`defaults()`). |

The build downloads Bravura (the SMuFL reference font, SIL OFL 1.1) and puts
`Bravura.json`, `Bravura.otf` and the licence in `bin/fonts/` next to the
executables (`cmake/ElaraDependencies.cmake`). Code points are checked against
the SMuFL specification's glyph list before a glyph is added to the enum.

Engraving rules (spacing, stems, beams, accidentals) and their constants are
recorded in ADR-0012, tablature, techniques and systems in ADR-0013, line
breaking and pages in ADR-0014. Inside
`src/`, a `StaffWriter` per staff (`standard_staff.cpp`, `tab_staff.cpp`)
reports the ink of each measure's columns and draws at the positions the
system (`system_layout.cpp`) gives them; `page_layout.cpp` measures every
measure, picks the line breaks and stacks the systems; tuplets are in ADR-0040.
Incremental relayout comes next in milestone 3 (`docs/roadmap.md`).

- **May depend on:** `elara_core`, the standard library (threads included) and
  yyjson (private: it never appears in public headers).
- **Must not include:** Qt, JUCE, `elara_io`, `elara_audio`.

Public headers live in `include/elara/notation/`, sources in `src/`, tests in
`tests/notation/`.
